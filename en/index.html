Open assistive technology, built through research.

AssistThings brings together several universities to build open assistive technology for people with disabilities, using Artificial Intelligence and the Internet of Things with affordable hardware and on-device processing.

See the projects Code on GitHub

Projects

Two prototypes in progress, with open code, hardware and documentation.

Smart Glasses: smart glasses for people with visual impairments

Glasses with a USB camera connected to a phone. Computer vision runs on the device itself, with no cloud, and results are spoken aloud. Already working on Android with real hardware.

  • Read textOn-device OCR with stable reading
  • PointReads the line your finger points to
  • ObjectsSays what it is and where it is
  • ColorsNames the color and says when it is unsure
  • FacesRecognizes registered people without storing photos
  • smartglasses-appFlutter · Kotlin · C (libuvc) · ML Kit · MediaPipe
  • smartglasses-iosSwiftUI · Apple Vision · OCR with local history

Bengala IoT: smart cane

Two ultrasonic sensors detect obstacles and warn with a vibration that grows stronger as the obstacle gets closer. An emergency button sends an SMS.

  • bengalaiot-esp32 · simulate on WokwiC++ · ESP32 · BLE · vibration
  • bengalaiot-dragonboard-410cPython · ultrasound · buzzer · SMS
  • bengalaiot-3d-printSTL parts for 3D printing
  • bengalaiot-raspberry-piin progressRaspberry Pi
  • bengalaiot-android-appin progressCompanion app

Principles

What guides the decisions behind every prototype.

Open

Code, hardware and documentation are free to reproduce and adapt.

Privacy

Processing happens on the device. Images and faces never go to the cloud.

Affordable

Cheap, easy-to-find parts: ESP32, UVC cameras, 3D printing.

People-centered

Features come from the real needs of the people who use them.

Partner institutions

Research done as a network of universities.

Partner universities will be announced soon.

How to contribute

  1. Pick a repository and read its README and docs/ folder.
  2. Open an issue to report a problem or suggest an idea.
  3. Send a pull request. Tests and accessibility (TalkBack, VoiceOver) matter a lot.

Researchers, students and people with disabilities who want to try the prototypes are very welcome.
